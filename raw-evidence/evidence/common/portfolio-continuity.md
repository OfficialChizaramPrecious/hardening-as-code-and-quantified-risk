# Advanced Portfolio Continuity Contract

The five projects are one increasing body of work. A candidate may improve an
earlier component, but may not replace it with an unrelated one-off solution
without documenting the incompatibility and migration.

## Required continuity record

Every submission must include `continuity-record.md` containing:

1. The previous-stage commit and exact component reused.
2. The interface consumed and any backward-compatible extension.
3. Evidence that the prior raw-to-result provenance remains intact.
4. A migration record for every incompatible change.
5. The component, schema, evidence, or decision record handed to the next stage.

## Anti-shortcut standard

- A report is not a substitute for executable work or structured evidence.
- Candidate IDs, flags, expected verdicts, exact counts, case markers, and
  private indicators may not be embedded in implementation logic.
- Every central result must be produced from the assigned input through a
  documented interface and retain an exact raw locator.
- Plausible alternative explanations must be tested against evidence and
  recorded, including why each was weakened or retained.
- Generated or transformed artifacts must record source paths, commands,
  versions, timestamps, and hashes.

## Track progression

### SOC Analysis

The hunt engine becomes the common evidence model; the honeypot and network
range add adapters; detection engineering consumes the verified behaviors; the
final case reuses the complete parser, hunt, provenance, and detection stack.

### Ethical Hacking

Scope-safe discovery becomes the common interface; exploit, IAM, directory,
and estate projects extend the same runtime identifier, evidence, cleanup, and
remediation model rather than starting from hard-coded target scripts.

### GRC

The typed control and evidence model flows through vendor assurance, audit,
technical treatment, and breach governance. Findings, exceptions, ownership,
deadlines, and decisions must remain traceable across stages.

## Failure conditions

- Replacing the previous component solely to embed the new variant answer.
- Losing raw evidence provenance during a migration.
- Claiming reuse when only filenames or report language were copied.
- Passing secret staff material, credentials, or another candidate's artifacts
  between stages.
